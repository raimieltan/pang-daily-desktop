# **01  /  How to use this storyboard**

The scene order below is the intended order of player experience. The chronology is deliberately nonlinear: the game opens with fragments of the crash, rewinds into the months before it, then returns to the present. Each scene card is designed to be usable by narrative, level design, cinematic, audio, and gameplay teams.

| EXPERIENCE TYPE | DESIGN USE |
| :---- | :---- |
| **PLAYABLE RACE** | The player drives. The race must change the story, reveal character, or generate evidence—not exist only as spectacle. |
| **PLAYABLE INVESTIGATION** | The player compares footage, telemetry, route geometry, or physical details. The answer must come from evidence already shown. |
| **CINEMATIC** | A fixed dramatic beat where player control would undermine pacing, clarity, or the fixed outcome. Use sparingly. |
| **HYBRID** | Player movement or inspection leads into a controlled cinematic reveal. |
| **MEMORY STITCH** | A transition links different dates through sound, motion, or an object. The join is intentional and later becomes a clue. |

## **The non-negotiable narrative rules**

* Ghost is not a hidden super-driver. The first reveal identifies the protagonist as the driver in the footage; the second proves the benchmark is a composite that no one completed in one run.  
* The benchmark must be concrete. Jolo’s edit combines the fastest recorded sectors and implies a synthetic combined pace. Dante markets that target as an unofficial legend, not as a formally certified record.  
* The crash is not solved by a magical clue. Road geometry, surviving camera footage, a damaged data logger, and a witness each add limited evidence. Together they make the lower-switchback explanation more credible than the imagined rival.  
* Jolo is loving but culpable in a human way. Give him a visible moment when he starts to tell the truth, sees what the opportunity means to his friend, and chooses silence.  
* Dante is an exploiter, not a mastermind. He deliberately misrepresents the benchmark and protects his event, but there is no evidence he arranged the crash.  
* Racing stays central in the middle. At least two playable drives after the crash must advance the investigation or dramatize the protagonist’s altered relationship with driving.  
* Confession is not absolution. The protagonist tells Jolo’s family what happened without asking them to forgive him. The ending changes his conduct, not the past.  
* Avoid repeating the theme after the image has landed. The final daylight drive should carry the meaning; explanatory dialogue should be minimal.

## **The Ghost Line: the rules the player must eventually understand**

1. Jolo records multiple practice passes on the same mountain route. Each pass contains good sectors and ordinary mistakes.  
2. He edits the best sector from Run A, the clean middle from Run B, and the fastest exit from Run C into one uninterrupted-looking clip. His original notes call it a “composite line study.”  
3. A sector/time overlay represents the fastest combined pace. The target is a constructed sum of sector performances, not a completed end-to-end lap time.  
4. Dante strips away the context and promotes the composite as the pace of an anonymous driver called Ghost. The community treats the synthetic benchmark like a real performance.  
5. The clues must be legible in retrospect: telemetry resets, different road/weather states, repeated engine audio at a join, and a road marker that changes between segments.

# **02  /  Character functions and race spine**

**THE PROTAGONIST**  Instinctive, underfunded, gifted. Reads weight transfer quickly but treats a successful correction as proof that he can always recover. Arc: from proving an ideal to responding to reality.

**JOLO**  Connector, camera operator, promoter of possibilities. Wants to build a garage/media outfit with his friend. His flaw is postponing hard truths until after the next opportunity.

**RUEL**  Mechanic and practical counterweight. Represents preparation, maintenance, and the limits of confidence. Keeps the damaged card and logger because evidence may matter later.

**DANTE**  Organizer who understands attention as currency. Knows the benchmark is a composite and chooses the marketable myth. Never secretly causes the crash.

**NICO**  Established, disciplined rival. Has real skill and real advantages. First exposes the difference between a spectacular sector and repeatable pace; later helps test the evidence.

**JOLO’S FAMILY**  Not a final obstacle to be overcome. They are the people owed the truth, and their grief cannot be converted into a reward for the protagonist’s honesty.

## **Playable race / drive inventory**

Every featured drive needs a distinct dramatic function. Reuse roads and locations where useful, but change the player’s relationship to them.

| ID | SEQUENCE | TIMELINE | PLAYER LEARNS |

**ACT 0**

# **The Missing Seconds**

**PROLOGUE  /  The player meets the crash before they understand it.**

*A short, fragmented opening that raises the central question without offering a clean explanation.*

## **S00  /  Cold Open: The Missing Seconds**

| CINEMATIC | CRASH NIGHT | Mountain road, after dark |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Place the player inside the sensory memory of the crash: rain, headlights, engine noise, Jolo’s voice. Introduce the Ghost Line as an image that feels authoritative before the player knows what it is.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  No conventional race objective. Use short sensory fragments from inside the car: the wet road, Jolo framing a shot, a corner entry, a flash of headlights between trees. Do not show a complete, explainable sequence.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Jolo says something that sounds like “drive the road.” The protagonist’s hands make a sharp correction. Light moves laterally across the windshield. Impact cuts sound and image. The player does not see a second car enter their lane.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Withhold the final approach speed, the road geometry, and the source of the headlights. The opening should not falsely establish contact or a deliberate attack.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  Black screen. A wrench strikes concrete in Ruel’s garage; the sound continues across the cut into the past.

## **S01  /  Match Cut: The Same Sound**

| MEMORY STITCH | MONTHS BEFORE | Ruel’s talyer, late afternoon |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Establish that the story will move between dates through emotional and sensory continuity, not random flashbacks.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player can look around a living garage: the protagonist’s aging car, Jolo’s camera gear, Ruel working on a stubborn part. A wrench hits the floor in the same rhythm as the crash cut.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Jolo teases the protagonist about the car’s latest improvised repair. Ruel checks a tire instead of joining the joke. The scene feels warm, specific, and ordinary.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  The same sound means something different here. The audience sees the people alive and together before understanding what the opening took away.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  A camera shutter becomes the starting beep of the first race.

**ACT I**

# **Before the Ghost**

**THE SCENE, THE FRIENDSHIP, THE MYTH**

*The player should first enjoy the world the protagonist is about to lose. The early races establish his gift and his dangerous misconception: if a correction works once, he can always make it work again.*

## **S02  /  The Talyer**

| PLAYABLE EXPLORATION | BEFORE CRASH | Ruel’s garage and nearby streets |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Make the garage a lived-in hub, not a menu. Set up the trio’s dynamic and the protagonist’s scarcity.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  Walk the small space, inspect the car, gather inexpensive parts, help Ruel with a quick repair, and check Jolo’s camera battery. Optional interactions reveal jokes, debts, and plans for a small garage/media business.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Ruel points out a brake and tire issue the protagonist is impatient to postpone. Jolo has lined up a race that might cover the repair bill. Nobody is foolish; they are all choosing between immediate needs and long-term stability.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Introduce Ruel’s habit of judging a car by its condition rather than its reputation. Establish the car’s recognizable steering correction in a safe, low-speed maneuver.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  Jolo announces that the entry fee is paid and the meet is waiting.

## **S03  /  Race 1: Nothing to Lose**

| PLAYABLE RACE | BEFORE CRASH | Industrial-edge loop / informal meet |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Prove the protagonist’s talent in a way the player feels through control, not exposition.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  A short race against better-funded cars. The player can win time through weight transfer, a late but controlled lift, and choosing a line that suits the protagonist’s modest setup. Avoid presenting a reckless move as universally correct.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  The protagonist wins a meaningful but modest purse. The crowd is surprised by how much pace he finds in an ordinary car. Jolo records the run and celebrates more than the protagonist does.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Show both the gift and the flaw: instinctive reading of the car, followed by a last-second correction that works on this surface. That correction will recur as a visual/audio signature.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  Dante approaches with congratulations and an invitation to a better event.

## **S04  /  The Invitation**

| CINEMATIC / HUB DIALOGUE | BEFORE CRASH | Meet parking area, then coffee shop |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Introduce Dante as a person who can turn a driver into a story.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player moves between small groups while Jolo negotiates access. Dante knows everyone, remembers names, and asks questions that reveal what the protagonist wants: better parts, respect, and a chance to stop starting from behind.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Dante offers opportunities, not charity. Entry fees, transport, and parts still cost money. Jolo is excited because networking is the one area where they can compete with wealthier drivers.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Dante notices the crowd’s reaction to the underdog story. His gift is reading the room; seed that he values a compelling narrative as much as lap times.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  A clean, expensive car arrives. Nico steps out and looks over the protagonist’s build.

## **S05  /  Race 2: Nico’s Lesson**

| PLAYABLE RACE | BEFORE CRASH | Private test loop / organized night meet |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Make Nico a legitimate rival and establish the difference between a fast sector and a repeatable run.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The protagonist can take an early sector or momentarily pull ahead, but Nico’s braking points, stable exits, and consistency build an advantage over the full run. The race should feel close, not rigged.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Nico wins without cheating and offers an unromantic explanation: the protagonist has pace, but he keeps spending grip he cannot afford. The protagonist hears condescension; the player should recognize some truth in it.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Nico’s car is better prepared, but his discipline is also real. Do not reduce his advantage to money alone. Record his habit of comparing repeated runs rather than celebrating a single best pass.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  Jolo tells the protagonist he will make a clip that proves what the car can do.

## **S06  /  A Line Study**

| HYBRID / MONTAGE | BEFORE CRASH | Mountain route, several practice days |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Show the original, innocent purpose of Ghost Line and make Jolo’s authorship concrete.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  A montage of player-driven practice fragments. The interface lets the player see Jolo’s camera position and occasional notes between runs. Each run ends with a small imperfection, restart, or changed condition.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Jolo selects a strong entry from one pass, a clean middle from another, and a fast exit from a third. On his working timeline he labels it “COMPOSITE LINE STUDY — BEST SECTORS / NOT ONE RUN.” He is proud of the edit and wants it to help his friend.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Make at least three source conditions visually distinct: a road marker is intact in one pass and damaged in another; asphalt reflectivity changes; telemetry resets at sector boundaries. None should be highlighted so strongly that the answer is obvious yet.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  The finished edit cuts cleanly to a continuous engine note. A message from Dante arrives asking to see the clip.

## **S07  /  Ghost Goes Public**

| CINEMATIC / SOCIAL FEED | BEFORE CRASH | Coffee shop / private racing group |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Define what the benchmark actually is and how it becomes misleading.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player scrolls through the clip’s circulation. Comments identify sector splits and compare the synthetic combined pace against their own runs. Dante’s promotional version removes Jolo’s title card and calls it the pace of an anonymous driver: Ghost.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Dante understands it is an edit but argues that the scene knows it is an unofficial legend. Jolo notices the label has changed, hesitates, then lets it spread because the attention is already opening doors.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  The community is competing against a combined best-sector target, not a verified end-to-end record. Establish that this is how Dante makes the myth measurable and profitable.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  A notification announces a Ghost Line challenge. The protagonist opens the clip and begins studying it.

## **S08  /  Race 3: Chase the Composite**

| PLAYABLE PRACTICE | BEFORE CRASH | Mountain route, dry conditions |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Make the benchmark seductive and show the protagonist beginning to mistake it for a promise.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player drives the route attempting to follow the overlay. The ghost path looks unnaturally clean, but the player can match one sector. A later sector demands a different entry rhythm and conditions than the previous one.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  The protagonist improves his own time, yet the combined target remains out of reach. He feels that he is nearly there rather than questioning whether the target represents one real drive.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Plant a brief engine-note repeat at a hidden edit point. The road marker changes between reference sectors. Avoid a UI popup explaining the inconsistency.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  Dante announces a public event where drivers will try to beat Ghost’s combined sector pace.

## **S09  /  Race 4: The Combined-Sector Challenge**

| PLAYABLE RACE / EVENT | BEFORE CRASH | Organized mountain-road challenge |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Turn the myth into social pressure. The player should see how an impossible target changes an ordinary event.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  Drivers attempt sector targets and compare a total built from best segments. The player can win a sector but not the combined pace. Other drivers argue about setup, tires, gearing, and whether Ghost is a professional.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Nico’s notes show that several drivers can match individual sections but no one reproduces the sequence at the advertised pace. Dante turns this failure into more hype: the benchmark becomes “unbeatable” precisely because no complete run can satisfy it.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  One leaderboard labels the target “combined best sectors” briefly before the promotional overlay replaces the wording with “GHOST TIME.” This is a fair, missable clue.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  After the event, Jolo sees the protagonist staring at the benchmark and begins to tell him something.

## **S10  /  Jolo’s Almost-Confession**

| CINEMATIC / INTIMATE DIALOGUE | BEFORE CRASH | Car interior, parked after the event |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Give Jolo a decisive moment of silence that the audience can reinterpret later.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player remains in the parked car. Jolo opens the project notes on his phone and starts: “There’s something about the clip I should’ve—”. He sees how excited the protagonist is about the next event and stops.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Jolo says they should focus on the car and the weather instead. The protagonist interprets this as nerves. Jolo pockets the phone, knowing the opportunity may disappear if he corrects the story.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  The audience sees the choice; the protagonist does not. Do not use ominous music or underline it as a confession. It should feel like an ordinary, regrettable hesitation.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  Dante calls with a larger offer and a deadline.

## **S11  /  The Offer Has a Price**

| PLAYABLE HUB / DIALOGUE | BEFORE CRASH | Garage, coffee shop, group chat |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Make the final event materially tempting rather than an arbitrary dare.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player checks repair estimates, event details, and Jolo’s outstanding commitments. The prize could clear immediate debts and pay for tires and proper repairs, but only if they perform well. Side obligations have accumulated because each prior opportunity seemed to justify one more expense.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Dante needs a headline event; Jolo needs money and momentum; the protagonist needs to prove the benchmark is within reach. Each character has a different reason to continue.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Dante’s wrongdoing is pressure through selective framing, not an explicit threat. Do not imply he arranged for another driver to interfere.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  Ruel asks to inspect the tires before they commit.

## **S12  /  The Weather Check**

| PLAYABLE PREPARATION / DIALOGUE | CRASH NIGHT, BEFORE EVENT | Ruel’s garage, then mountain staging area |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Put the warning in front of the player clearly so the crash is not caused by missing information.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player inspects tires, brakes, and the forecast with Ruel. The available tires are worn and not suited to the deteriorating wet conditions. Jolo says a reference line cannot tell them how the road will grip tonight.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Ruel recommends withdrawing. The protagonist refuses, convinced he can adapt on the fly and that the event is their only chance. Jolo can still tell the full truth about the composite, but the moment passes.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  The scene must be emotionally credible, not a lecture. The protagonist is not ignorant of risk; he believes his talent will let him manage it.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  Headlights enter the mist. The event begins.

## **S13  /  Race 5: The Last Night Run**

| PLAYABLE RACE INTO AUTHORED CINEMATIC | CRASH NIGHT | Mountain road, rain and mist |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Make the fatal run the collision of gameplay, myth, and character choice. Do not frame it as a random accident or a conspiracy.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player drives the opening sectors while crowds and camera lights fall behind. The Ghost overlay becomes more insistent as the protagonist chases its timing. The surface changes; grip falls away; Jolo’s warnings are audible but compete with engine and road noise.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  At the key bend, the protagonist imitates the reference entry instead of responding to the wet surface. A sharp correction destabilizes the car. Headlights flash through the trees from the lower switchback. Impact. The player is not offered a fake last-second button prompt that pretends the fixed outcome is avoidable.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  This scene establishes the actual decisions: event pressure, unsuitable conditions, late braking, and overcorrection. The headlights are real but not in the protagonist’s lane. Preserve the last seconds for later reconstruction.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  Sound drops out. A single pulse of Jolo’s camera recording continues into the next act.

**ACT II**

# **The Return**

**GRIEF, RUMORS, AND THE NEED FOR A CULPRIT**

*The protagonist returns to the scene looking for an outside cause. The middle must still contain meaningful driving: racing now reveals trauma, inconsistent evidence, and the gap between peak pace and repeatability.*

## **S14  /  Waking Without a Finish**

| CINEMATIC | MONTHS AFTER CRASH | Clinic / rented room |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Establish the physical recovery and the missing memory without turning injury into a convenient total blackout.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The protagonist wakes to practical details: stiffness, messages, bills, people asking questions, and silence where Jolo should be. His memory retains sensory shards rather than a clean sequence.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  The official account describes a single-car crash. He remembers another car or lights near the bend, but cannot say what it did. The uncertainty becomes a space for blame.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Avoid making the memory loss medically absolute. He remembers enough to be haunted; he lacks the sequence needed to be certain.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  A message from Ruel says the car and the camera card are still at the garage.

## **S15  /  The Card and the Logger**

| PLAYABLE EXPLORATION | AFTER CRASH | Ruel’s garage |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Bring the car back as a physical archive. Ruel’s decision to preserve the damaged camera card and logger becomes important without seeming clairvoyant.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player walks around the repaired but scarred car, inspects the damaged camera mount, and handles a data logger that survived the impact. Ruel has kept the parts because Jolo’s family might need answers.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Ruel does not immediately interpret the files. He says the car can tell them some things, but not everything. The protagonist is more interested in identifying the other driver than in looking at his own inputs.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Let the damage and missing camera seconds be visible. The evidence is incomplete from the start.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  A small local meet is announced. The protagonist decides to race again.

## **S16  /  Race 6: First Return**

| PLAYABLE RACE | AFTER CRASH | Controlled industrial loop / morning meet |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Show that the protagonist’s relationship with driving has changed before he can explain it.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player enters a short, controlled course to prove that the crash has not taken his ability. A headlight reflection flashes across a barrier; for a moment the sound and camera movement recall the mountain bend. The protagonist overbrakes, loses rhythm, and finishes poorly.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  He tries to disguise the result as rust or an uncompetitive setup. Ruel sees that the issue is not simply pace. The protagonist is both more cautious and more desperate to force a clean run.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Make the trauma visible in racing behavior rather than a generic nightmare montage. The race is not a triumph; it begins the investigation.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  A spectator mentions that another car was supposedly near them on the mountain.

## **S17  /  The Story in the Parking Lot**

| PLAYABLE DIALOGUE / INVESTIGATION | AFTER CRASH | Coffee shop and gas-station meet |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Introduce competing rumors without letting rumor function as evidence.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player questions racers, watches old clips, and compares accounts. One says Ghost was a professional; another claims the driver was at the crash; a third says the footage is years old.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Each account contains a detail that cannot fit with the others. People have reasons to believe the version that flatters their own status, protects the event, or gives the tragedy a clear culprit.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Use repeated but conflicting details as a clue to social mythmaking. No witness should suddenly know the answer.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  Nico tells the protagonist that he tried to reproduce the Ghost Line and failed.

## **S18  /  Race 7: Nico Repeats the Test**

| PLAYABLE TEST DRIVE | AFTER CRASH | Mountain route / recorded practice session |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Use a rival’s evidence to undermine the benchmark without making Nico a detective who solves everything offscreen.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player drives selected sectors with Nico’s notes visible between attempts. Nico can match one section, then loses time when the next segment behaves differently. Conditions, grip, and entry speed never line up into one repeatable run.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Nico admits the issue is not simply horsepower or a secret technique. His sector notes suggest the sequence is too clean and too consistent for a single pass, but he cannot yet prove why.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Seed the first explicit testable clue: a sector split and engine RPM trace reset where the video appears continuous. Nico shares the notes because he would rather know the truth than preserve his pride.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  A gear change in the recording bridges into a memory from before the crash.

## **S19  /  Memory Stitch: The Wrong Tuesday**

| MEMORY STITCH / SHORT PLAYABLE FLASHBACK | MIXED DATES | Garage and route, pre-crash fragments |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Teach the audience how to read the nonlinear form: emotional continuity is not proof of chronological continuity.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  A conversation about a repair continues across a cut. The car has a different wheel in the next shot; Jolo wears the same shirt in scenes that were filmed on different days; weather shifts while dialogue seems uninterrupted.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  The protagonist reconstructs memories by association. The player can notice details that do not line up, but the scene never suggests supernatural time displacement.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  This is the formal mirror of the composite edit. The memory is not fabricated wholesale; separate true moments have been joined into a false continuity.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  A cut from a remembered laugh lands on Dante laughing in the present.

## **S20  /  Dante’s Half-Truth**

| CINEMATIC / CONFRONTATION | AFTER CRASH | Back room at an event venue |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Clarify Dante’s incentive while leaving unresolved questions where evidence is genuinely missing.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The protagonist asks who arranged the night’s drivers and why no one will identify Ghost. Dante says he never hired anyone to force them off the road and never intended a death. He admits the benchmark’s source was uncertain.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  Dante claims everyone understood that Ghost was a legend. The protagonist recognizes the evasion: Dante sold the fiction as a challenge because an “unbeatable” target keeps people returning.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Do not confirm Dante’s innocence in every conceivable respect; establish only that there is no evidence he caused the crash. His complicity is the deliberate marketing of a dangerous false premise.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  Dante refuses to provide the original files. Ruel remembers an old backup drive.

**ACT III**

# **The Ghost Is You**

**FIRST REVELATION, THEN THE REAL REVELATION**

*The first discovery should feel emotionally complete for a moment. The second revelation should then transform the meaning of the first, not merely add another fact.*

## **S21  /  The Backup Folder**

| PLAYABLE INVESTIGATION | AFTER CRASH | Ruel’s garage, computer bench |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Let the player find the archive by inspecting the evidence rather than having Ruel announce the answer.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  Search Jolo’s old project folders, phone backups, notes, and exported clips. Several files are corrupted or re-encoded. The player assembles a chain of source footage using camera angles, dashboard reflections, and dates.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  One folder carries the original Ghost Line project title. The player can preview raw passes and compare them with the circulating clip, but the actual driver identity remains a hypothesis until the next scene.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Raw metadata is partial; the story must not pretend every digital file survived intact. A recognizable steering correction and interior reflection are stronger than a filename alone.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  Ruel overlays two clips and asks the protagonist to watch the driver’s hands.

## **S22  /  Reveal 1: Behind the Camera**

| HYBRID / EVIDENCE REVEAL | AFTER CRASH | Garage, monitors and damaged car |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Reveal that the protagonist is Ghost, using multiple independent signs.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player aligns the circulating benchmark with raw footage. A small steering correction, throttle lift, dashboard reflection, passenger-seat camera angle, and engine note line up with the protagonist’s own archived practice runs.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  The clip came from the protagonist’s car; Jolo was recording. The driver he has been chasing is himself. The protagonist hears his own voice, then Jolo laughing as he asks for another attempt.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  Do not reveal the composite nature in this scene. Let the player temporarily believe the simpler explanation: Jolo uploaded one extraordinary run anonymously.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  The clip loops, and the apparently continuous engine note repeats at a point the player has already heard.

## **S23  /  Voice in the Archive**

| CINEMATIC / EMOTIONAL BEAT | AFTER CRASH WITH PRE-CRASH FOOTAGE | Garage, after closing |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Let the first reveal land emotionally before the second undercuts it.

# **What Happened on the Mountain**

**THE EVIDENCE RULES OUT THE STORY HE WANTED**

*The investigation does not produce perfect certainty. It produces the most coherent account—and removes the unsupported rival from the center of the protagonist’s story.*

## **S27  /  The Lower Switchback**

| PLAYABLE INVESTIGATION | CRASH NIGHT RECONSTRUCTED | Map table and mountain overlook |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Explain the headlights through real route geography rather than declaring the memory imaginary.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player compares the surviving dash footage with a route map and vantage points. A witness who was leaving a roadside stop confirms another car used the lower switchback that night, but cannot place it near the crash.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  The road folds back on itself. Lights from the lower section slide through gaps in the trees and appear to move alongside the protagonist’s car for a moment. The terrain separates the vehicles; the other car never enters their lane in the surviving footage.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  The headlights were real; the assumed threat was not supported. The witness is limited and imperfect. Avoid a convenient all-seeing bystander.

**EXIT / TRANSITION**  Nico marks the fatal bend on the map. Ruel brings out the recovered logger.

| PLAYABLE EVIDENCE RECONSTRUCTION | CRASH NIGHT RECONSTRUCTED | Garage, logger and partial camera file |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Let the player reconstruct the driver’s actions using data while respecting the limits of damaged evidence.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  The player syncs surviving speed, braking, and steering traces with the partial camera file. The last video seconds are missing, but the data continues far enough to show approach speed, delayed braking, and a sharp correction as stability is lost.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  The data fits the wet road and the car’s departure from the road. It does not show a collision or an evasive input consistent with a nearby vehicle. Jolo’s warning is audible shortly before the final gap.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  The logger cannot prove every detail. It does, however, support a coherent sequence that fits better than the theory of a rival forcing them off the road.

| PLAYABLE SYNTHESIS | PRESENT | Garage, late night |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

**PURPOSE**  Make the player perform the final reasoning rather than receive a single explanatory monologue.

**PLAYER EXPERIENCE**  Place four pieces of evidence together: source video proves the protagonist drove the Ghost footage; cut points prove the benchmark is composite; route geometry explains the lights; logger data supports excessive speed and overcorrection on a wet corner.

**DRAMATIC BEATS**  The player can test each conclusion against the surviving evidence. The reconstruction does not answer every possible question, but the rival theory no longer explains the facts better than the ordinary one.

**CLUES / CONTINUITY**  This is the game’s evidentiary standard: specific clues, corroboration, honest limits. The protagonist’s memory preserved danger but supplied an unsupported cause.

**ACT APPENDIX**

# **Production Notes & Continuity Locks**

**GUIDANCE FOR THE NEXT PASS**

*These are implementation notes to keep the storyboard coherent during scene writing, level design, cinematics, and gameplay prototyping.*

## **Mystery clue ladder**

| STAGE | PLAYER CAN ESTABLISH |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Early / easy to miss** | At a join in the Ghost clip, a small engine RPM fluctuation repeats. A road marker or patch of asphalt appears different in another sector. |
| **Midgame / testable** | Nico’s sector notes and the telemetry reset do not fit a continuous pass. His failed reproduction shows the benchmark is not merely a secret driving technique. |
| **Reveal 1** | Camera angle, dashboard reflection, steering signature, engine note, and Jolo’s voice independently connect the source footage to the protagonist’s car. |
| **Reveal 2** | Timestamps, three source files, reset telemetry, inconsistent conditions, and Jolo’s original “composite line study” note prove the clip is assembled. |
| **Crash reconstruction** | Route geography explains the apparent parallel headlights; witness testimony places a car on the lower switchback; logger data supports speed too high for the wet corner and a destabilizing correction. |
| **Conclusion** | The evidence does not prove every second, but the rival-driver theory lacks support. The protagonist accepts the account that best fits the facts. |

## **Cinematic and audio motifs**

* Wrench on concrete → gear shift → click of a turn signal. Use the same sound to bridge periods; later reveal that a smooth transition can hide a gap.  
* Engine note continuity. The crossfade that makes Ghost appear continuous should have a tiny, repeatable audio artifact. It is a clue, not a horror sting.  
* Headlights through trees. First show the lights as a frightening flash in the cold open; later show the lower switchback geography that changes their meaning.  
* Camera framing. Jolo’s passenger-seat footage is a recurring visual language of friendship. The first reveal makes it evidence; the emotional playback restores its human meaning.  
* Ghost overlay. At first it appears authoritative and clean; after the composite reveal the player can see the joins; in the ending it appears briefly but no longer controls the protagonist’s choices.

## **Dialogue restraint**

* Keep explanations short when the player has just seen the proof. Characters can verify evidence but should not restate the theme in full.  
* Jolo’s almost-confession should be a small social moment, not a dramatic villain reveal. The player understands it fully only after seeing the source notes.  
* Dante should concede the business logic behind the myth, not confess to engineering the crash.  
* The protagonist’s confession to Jolo’s family should be plain, factual, and unadorned. Do not write it as a plea for forgiveness.  
* The final line or exchange should be about checking the car and responding to the road—not summarizing the whole plot.

## **Pacing and quality gates**

* Do not let the post-crash investigation go too long without a playable drive. S16, S18, and S26 are required story-driving sequences, not optional side content.  
* Separate the two Ghost revelations with an emotional beat and a new question: first “the driver is me,” then “that complete run never happened.”  
* The crash reconstruction must use at least three evidence sources and state the limits of each. Do not offer a meaningless last-second QTE during S13.  
* No ending branch should award forgiveness, a championship trophy, or a “Ghost beaten” achievement. Before scripting dialogue, playtest whether people can suspect the composite before S25 without being handed the answer.

## **Vertical-slice build order**

For an efficient first playable, prove the story’s distinctive mechanics in this order before expanding every hub and event.

| PROTOTYPE | PASS CONDITION |
| :---- | :---- |
| **1\. Driving identity** | Build the garage hub and R1. Prove the car feels underfunded but responsive; the late correction must read clearly. |
| **2\. Synthetic benchmark** | Build the Ghost overlay, sector targets, and R3/R4 event flow. The target must be legible as a combined-sector pace without giving away the edit. |
| **3\. Crash-to-return contrast** | Prototype the fragmentary cold open, authored crash sequence, and S16 return race. Reuse sound and light motifs consistently. |
| **4\. Evidence loop** | Build the source-footage alignment, composite timeline, sector trials, route map, and logger sync. Every conclusion must be supported by visible evidence. |
| **5\. Ending mechanic** | Prototype S34 with no timer or medal. The Ghost overlay recedes when the player chooses the line appropriate to present conditions. |

**END STATE  The protagonist does not defeat Ghost. He stops treating Ghost as an obligation. The road remains uncertain; his responsibility is to meet it as it is.**
